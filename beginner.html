Day 1 in detail

Day 1 on the island · a timeline walkthrough

The most important thing on Day 1 is not rushing progress, but learning the map and where resources are. Here is a step-by-step guide in game-time order.

🌅 Morning (arrival — before noon): create your character + the tutorial

Pick a good name (you cannot rename it later in game) and choose your look. Follow the NPC guidance through the basic tutorial — moving, interacting, opening your bag, and viewing the map. There is no time-penalty mechanic, so take your time. The tutorial introduces you to the main NPCs and facilities in the town center.

☀️ Noon — afternoon: explore the map + mark key locations

Tour the three main areas: Town Center (the shop street, Massimo's café, bug-catching mentor Naniwa), the Suburbs (the pet adoption center run by Mrs Joan, gardening mentor Blanc), and the Fishing Village (fishing mentor Vanya, the docks). Be sure to mark Meadow Lake — fluorite ore and treant roots respawn there every day, making it a must-visit resource spot.
💡 Tip: long-press anywhere on the map to drop a custom marker, which greatly speeds up future trips.

🌤️ Afternoon — evening: finish villager quests → DG Lv.2

Open the map and find all villager quests marked with icons. Day 1 quests are usually simple: deliver items, chat, or gather basic materials. Each quest rewards 1,000-3,000 gold + contribution medals + Wishing Stars. Complete 2-3 quests to reach DG Lv.2.
⚠️ Do not waste your Wishing Stars! Save them for the expansion discount.

🌙 Evening — night: 🎣 unlock Fishing

After DG Lv.2, the NPC Vanya visits you automatically, gifts you a fishing rod, and unlocks the Fishing hobby — the first production hobby of the game. Start practicing in rivers and ponds — sardines (40G), goldfish (50G), sea bass (55G), crucian carp (80G). Save every fish worth ≤100G and never sell them! Once you unlock cooking, they will be worth 3-5x more.

🌙 Before bed (after 23:00)

① Tidy your bag and keep at least 5-8 empty slots for tomorrow's foraged items
② Store low-value fish and, if you have any >100G fish, sell them to Albert Jr. tomorrow
③ If you have already reached DG Lv.3 (by doing enough quests), unlock Gardening from Blanc and plant potatoes right before logging off — they take 2 hours, so they will be ready to harvest when you wake up
④ Check tomorrow's weather: rain saves you from watering, while sunny days mean you water in the morning

📋 Day 1 quick checklist: ✅ Explored the whole map and marked Meadow Lake, the café, and the pet center   ✅ Reached DG Lv.2 and unlocked Fishing   ✅ Caught at least 5-10 fish and stored them   ✅ Spent zero Wishing Stars   ✅ Know where to find Albert Jr. to sell things

Day 2 & Day 3 · a day-by-day guide

📅 Day 2: unlock Gardening and start farming for gold

① Harvest + water (~3 min)

If you planted potatoes last night, they are ready to harvest now. Water all crops (automatically skipped on rainy days). Hit each fruit tree only once — the first hit always drops fruit, the next two only give wood, so hit once and move on; it refreshes in 2 minutes.

② Rush DG Lv.3 → 🌱 Gardening

Complete today's 5 villager quests. Once you reach DG Lv.3, find NPC Blanc (near the town center) right away to unlock Gardening. Buy potato seeds — the best beginner crop, earning 3x more than tomatoes. For comparison: potatoes cost 35G each and tomatoes 12G each, but potatoes pair with fish and chips to create 310G of value.

③ Make a planting plan

Potatoes ripen in 2 hours → rotate them quickly during the day (3-4 harvests possible). Blueberries ripen in 6 hours → plant before bed, harvest on waking. Leave one plot for wheat (a must from DG Lv.5 onward). Do not buy strawberry or grape seeds — selling them raw beats making jam; reconsider only once your Cooking level is higher.

④ Keep fishing to stock ingredients

Fish in rivers and ponds for crucian carp (80G, 350G+ as fried carp) and common carp (65G). If ocean fishing spots are open, you can go there too. Remember the 100G dividing line: store anything ≤100G for cooking, and sell anything >100G straight to Albert Jr.

⑤ Gather free resources

Run a lap around the map collecting all respawn points: berry bushes (town outskirts), mushrooms (forest area), and wild fruit (randomly placed). Free = zero cost = 100% pure-profit ingredients. Also keep an eye out for black truffles (autumn · sparkling spots on the forest floor) — 200G each, a core late-game ingredient.

📅 Day 3: cooking takes off and your money loop is established

① Rush DG Lv.5 → 🍳 the key choice

Complete all 5 villager quests. At Lv.5 you receive your first hobby expansion ticket — one of the most important choices in the game. You have three options: Cooking (Massimo · café), Bug-catching (Naniwa · town center), and Bird-watching (Bailey J). We strongly recommend Cooking first — see the details below.

② Why you must pick Cooking first? (let the numbers speak)

Bug-catching and bird-watching are pure-consumption hobbies — they take time but produce no gold. Cooking immediately builds your money engine:
→ 4 blueberries (64G cost) → blueberry jam 170-320G (profit ×2.7-5)
→ 1 crucian carp + flour (100G cost) → fried carp 350-700G (profit ×2.5-6)
→ any fish + potato (75G cost) → fish and chips 310-620G (profit ×3-7)
Without Cooking, you can only earn by selling raw ingredients — at most 2,000-3,000G a day; with Cooking, 5,000-8,000G a day is easy.

③ First high-profit recipe: blueberry jam

Learn the blueberry jam recipe from Massimo (at the café). 4 blueberries → 1 jar of jam. One-star sells for 170G, two-star 255G, three-star 320G. The higher your Cooking level, the better your odds of high-star results. Blueberries take 6 hours to grow — plant before bed, harvest on waking, and make jam during the day: a perfect schedule.

④ Second recipe: fish and chips + intro to the four-stove method

Learn the fish and chips recipe (any fish ×1 + potato ×1 → 310G). This is the king of value, with a ROI as high as 313%.
🔥 The four-stove method: place 4 stoves in a square and stand in the middle to manage 4 dishes at once. That is 4x output with overlapping cooking times. In the beginner phase, run all 4 stoves on blueberry jam or fish and chips.

⑤ Your daily loop takes shape (and stays like this from now on)

Morning harvest and plant (rotate potatoes + plant blueberries before bed) → late morning gather free ingredients (berries, mushrooms, herbs) → noon fish (high-value ocean or stockpile low-value river fish) → afternoon batch-cook on four stoves (jam + fish and chips) → evening finish 5 villager quests → night sell all finished goods to Albert Jr. → before bed plant potatoes/wheat/blueberries. From this day on, you are in a steady daily money rhythm.

Resource management

What is worth spending on, and what is not?

The game has three core resources: Wishing Stars (scarce), gold (earnable), and time (finite). Each has priorities — spend wisely and you get twice the results for half the effort.

⭐ Wishing Star spending priority

🥇 Top priority: bag & storage expansion (first 5 times)

The first 5 bag expansions (+10 slots) and storage expansions (+50 slots) each cost only 10 Wishing Stars (normally 25 — a 40% discount). This is the best investment in the game — bag space directly affects gathering and fishing efficiency. Buy them from NPC Ka Ching (across from plot 8 in the northwest suburbs). In total you need 50 stars = +50 bag slots +250 storage slots. From the 6th purchase it jumps to 25 stars each, so stop buying after the discount ends.

🥈 Second priority: hobby unlocks & Cooking upgrades

Fishing (auto at Lv.2) and Gardening (auto at Lv.3) cost no Wishing Stars. Cooking (Lv.5, via a hobby expansion ticket) is the first real decision — and a must-pick. Later, bug-catching (Lv.8) and bird-watching (Lv.11) each need a ticket. The higher your Cooking level, the better your high-star odds — the same ingredients earn 50-100% more.

🥉 Third priority: functional buildings & furniture

Extra stoves (one more stove = 1.3x cooking efficiency), more farmland, and functional storage furniture. These directly boost your earning power — this is "spend money to make money" investing. One extra stove means 3-5 more dishes a day, roughly +1,000-1,500G/day.

💡 Emergency uses & places to never spend

Emergency: 1 star for a firecracker → sell to Albert Jr. = 1,100G (do not do this often; Wishing Stars are far more precious than gold)
❌ Never spend on: speeding up crop growth (2 hours is not worth 1 star), buying base ingredients (you get them free from farming/fishing/gathering), pure decoration (consider only after DG Lv.15 advanced building), or speeding up pet/hobby progress

Mistake-avoidance guide

The 11 most common beginner mistakes

Each one was learned the hard way by veteran players — knowing them in advance saves you three days of detours

❌ 1. Wasting Wishing Stars

The first 5 bag/storage expansions cost only 10 stars (normally 25) — always buy them all: 50 stars for +50 bag slots +250 storage slots. Wishing Stars are scarce, and wasting them means spending even more stars to recover later. Never buy speed-up items or decorations!

❌ 2. Picking bug-catching or bird-watching first

This is the "poor for three years" route. Without the money-making power of Cooking, early gold is extremely tight: bug-catching and bird-watching produce no gold, so you live off selling raw ingredients, and DG leveling slows because you cannot afford quests. Cooking is the only hobby that builds a positive economic loop early on.

❌ 3. Cooking high-value fish

Tuna (300), pufferfish (450), and trout (250) should be sold straight to Albert Jr. Cooking them wastes their value, and the finished dish often sells for less than the raw fish. Remember the 100G dividing line: above it, sell directly; below it, save for cooking. The one exception: king crab cooked into steamed king crab with the full set of ingredients.

❌ 4. Using the rainbow bouquet on rainbow weather

Rainbow weather (a special weather event) already has a very high rare-species spawn rate, so stacking a rainbow bouquet on top exceeds the cap and is a waste. Correct use: save the rainbow bouquet for normal weather, turning it into a mini rainbow day. Likewise, avoid stacking other rarity-boosting items during special weather.

❌ 5. Selling raw ingredients instead of processing them

4 blueberries sold raw = 64G; made into jam = 170-320G. Any low-value fruit → jam, and any low-value fish → fish and chips / fried carp, for profit that at least doubles. Never sell raw ingredients unless they are already valuable (>100G fish, strawberries, grapes).

❌ 6. Investing in house decorating too early

In the early game, all gold should go into seeds, expansions, stoves, and cooking materials — production tools that generate money. Decorating is pure spending; wait until DG Lv.15 unlocks advanced building mode, when it is more efficient and offers more choices. An empty house early on does not affect your income.

❌ 7. Hitting a fruit tree three times

The first hit always drops fruit; the next two only give wood and branches. Hit each tree once and move on — it refreshes in 2 minutes and you can hit it again. Maximizing the fruit-per-energy ratio = maximizing free income. Tap each tree as you pass during a lap and you will bank an extra 200-300G in free fruit.

❌ 8. Planting only one crop

Villager quests often ask you to deliver X of crop Y. If your field is all potatoes and a quest asks for 3 tomatoes, you are stuck — or forced to buy them at a high price. Keep 2-3 crop types growing at once: potatoes (main money-maker) + tomatoes/carrots (quest reserve) + wheat (long-term investment).

❌ 9. Selling piecemeal instead of stockpiling

Running to sell after every single fish is extremely inefficient. Instead, stockpile a batch of low-value fish (10-20), cook them all at once on four stoves, then sell everything to Albert Jr. in one trip. Batching = less travel time = more time for other money-making activities.

❌ 10. Using Wishing Stars to speed up crops

Potatoes take 2 hours and wheat 4 — just wait. One Wishing Star buys a firecracker worth 1,100G, while a single speed-up saves only 2 hours; in those 2 hours you could fish or gather more than 1,100G. Trading Wishing Stars for time is always a loss early on.

❌ 11. Ignoring how weather and seasons affect fishing

Different fish appear only in specific weather or seasons. On sunny days fish the ocean for tuna, on rainy days fish the river for eel, and in autumn search the forest for black truffles. Check the forecast before heading out and decide what to focus on today. Do not go to the wrong place at the wrong time — it wastes time and yields no high-value fish.

Daily checklist

10 minutes a day · the efficient money checklist

A standard routine to run after every login, optimized for the beginner phase (DG 1-7). Expected yield 3,000-5,000 gold per round, compressing to 8 minutes with practice.

① Harvest + plant (~2 min)Fixed income

Harvest all ripe crops → replant. Rotate potatoes during the day (2h growth, multiple harvests), plant blueberries whenever you have seeds (6h, plant before bed), and plant wheat whenever you have seeds (4h, long-term investment). Remember: you only water on sunny days; rain skips it automatically.

② Free gathering sweep (~2 min)Zero-cost profit

Follow a fixed route: home → Meadow Lake (fluorite ore + treant roots, refresh daily) → forest area (mushrooms + berry bushes) → town outskirts (tap fruit trees). Autumn focus: look for sparkling spots on the forest floor → black truffles (200G each, a core late-game cooking ingredient). Three mushrooms roast into a 120G dish — zero-cost pure profit.

③ Fishing (~2 min)Flexible income

Quickly cast at high-value spots: the ocean (summer · tuna 300G + pufferfish 450G) and the river (crucian carp 80G → fried carp 350G). Store anything ≤100G for cooking and mark anything >100G as to sell. If you get lucky and land a high-value fish (trout 250G+), this step's yield can double.

④ Four-stove batch cooking (~2 min)Profit core

Check your storage and pick one main product to batch: blueberry jam (4 blueberries → 170G+, first choice when stocked), fish and chips (fish + potato → 310G+, on days with lots of low-value fish), fried carp (crucian carp + flour → 350G+, on crucian-carp days). Run all 4 stoves at once — 4 finished dishes in 2 minutes.

⑤ Villager quests + selling (~2 min)Quest rewards

Open the map and check today's 5 villager quests — if you stockpiled common items earlier, many can be handed in immediately. When done, find the wandering merchant Albert Jr. (he roams the whole map, so watch for him) and batch-sell all finished goods and any >100G fish. Hold an item to sell in bulk. Daily quests reward 5,000-7,000G plus Wishing Stars — the single highest-yield item on this checklist.

🚀 6 tips to level faster

① Never miss the 5 daily quests

This is the biggest source of contribution medals. If you miss even one day of all 5, your DG progress is pushed back a full day. Make accepting quests the first thing you do each login.

② Stockpile common quest items in advance

Keep in storage: potatoes ×10, wheat ×10, blueberry jam ×5, fish and chips ×5, vegetable medley ×5, and various fish ×5. When a quest arrives you can hand it in instantly instead of rushing to farm/fish/cook. Grow multiple crop types to maximize quest coverage.

③ Cooking synergy — earn medals while making money

Cooking produces not just gold, but contribution medals with every dish. Four-stove batch cooking = 4x medal efficiency. Cooking is the only hobby that produces both gold and medals — which is why picking it first matters just as much for fast leveling.

④ Prioritize the main story

Main quests reward far more medals than dailies and push your progress permanently. When a main-quest icon appears, complete it first — it is a one-time, non-repeatable chunk of experience.

⑤ Higher-star products = more medals

Cooking/fishing/bug-catching results at ★★ or ★★★ quality grant more contribution medals than one-star results. Higher hobby level → higher crit rate → more medals → faster DG leveling. A positive loop.

⑥ Do not skip seemingly useless quests

Some quests look low-reward (e.g. only 1,000G), but they all grant contribution medals. Medal value cannot be measured in gold — every quest is one step of DG progress, and skipping any of them delays unlocking new features.

Must-knows

Mechanics every beginner must know

🏠 What is the DG level?

The Guild level (Development Guild) — the core progression system of the game. Level up by earning contribution medals from villager quests and the main story. Each level unlocks new features, hobbies, or areas. DG level = your game progress.

⭐ How to use Wishing Stars?

Earn them from villager quests. Priority: first 5 expansions (10 stars each) → hobby expansion tickets → in a cash crunch, 1 star for a firecracker sold at 1,100 gold. Do not splurge on decorations or speed-up items.

🕐 When does the server reset?

At 6:00 in-game time (check your watch to confirm the local offset). Daily quests, shop stock, and resource spots (fluorite ore / treant roots) all refresh at this time.

🍳 What does leveling hobbies do?

It raises the star quality of your output. For example, 1-star blueberry jam sells for 170 while 3-star sells for 320 — same ingredients, nearly double the price. The higher the hobby level, the better your odds of critting a high-star result.

🛒 Bag/storage expansion

Find NPC Ka Ching (across from plot 8 in the northwest suburbs). The first 5 expansions cost only 10 Wishing Stars (normally 25). DG levels 2/7/13/17/19 also grant free expansions. Always buy out the discounted ones first.

💰 Where is Albert Jr.?

The wandering merchant roams the whole map with no fixed spot. He pays more than shops. Hold an item to sell in bulk. Watch for him during your daily map laps.
